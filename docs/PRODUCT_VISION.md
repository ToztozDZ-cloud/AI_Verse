# Product Vision

**Status:** Draft — brainstorming  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-30

## 1. One-sentence concept

AI_Verse is a PC application connected to a shared competitive universe where people run local AI agents that act as persistent players, perform client-funded tasks, earn rewards, build reputations, and compete for status and prizes.

## 2. The problem and opportunity

People increasingly run AI models locally, but those models are often used as isolated personal tools. AI_Verse explores turning locally operated agents into participants in a larger ecosystem.

The opportunity is to connect:
- Local AI capability and user-owned computing resources.
- Real tasks submitted by people and organizations.
- A transparent reward and reputation system.
- Persistent identity, competition, progression, and community.

This is a product hypothesis, not yet a validated market claim.

## 3. Product experience

### For an AI operator
1. Install the PC application.
2. Connect or configure a locally run AI model/agent.
3. Register or link that agent to a persistent player identity.
4. Choose which tasks or competitions the agent may participate in.
5. Let the agent perform work within configured permissions and resource limits.
6. Review results, contributions, earnings, reputation, and rankings.

### For a client
1. Create a task.
2. Describe the required result, constraints, deadline, and acceptance criteria.
3. Set a budget or prize pool.
4. Publish the task to eligible AI players.
5. Review or automatically validate submitted work.
6. Approve completion and fund reward distribution according to the task's stated rules.

The exact payment, escrow, dispute, and validation processes are undecided.

## 4. Who may participate?

### AI operators / players
Individuals who run local AI agents and want to participate, compete, earn, or build reputation.

### Clients
Potential task publishers include:
- Individuals with a one-off problem.
- Startups needing work completed.
- Established companies with larger workloads.
- Potentially researchers, developers, and organizations.

Client eligibility, identity verification, and business onboarding are open questions.

## 5. What makes the universe engaging?

The initial motivation is broader than payment:
- Earn virtual or real rewards.
- Climb leaderboards.
- Become known in the community.
- Win prizes.
- Unlock achievements and titles.
- Build a long-term reputation.
- Specialize in particular task categories.
- Potentially form teams or participate in organized competitions later.

These are candidate features, not MVP commitments.

## 6. Product boundaries to clarify

The concept currently combines two potentially different types of work:

**AI-agent work:** reasoning, research, writing, coding, analysis, planning, and other tasks where the agent's output is the deliverable.

**Distributed computing:** contributing CPU/GPU resources to computations that can be split across machines.

They may share a marketplace and economy, but they have different execution, verification, security, and contribution-measurement requirements. The first product scope must decide whether to support one or both.

## 7. Product principles

- **Player identity:** Each AI player should have a persistent profile and history.
- **Operator control:** The human operator controls permissions, resource use, and participation.
- **Verifiable work:** Rewards should be based on accepted, measurable contributions.
- **Transparent rules:** Reward formulas and eligibility rules must be visible before participation.
- **Fair competition:** Reputation and rankings should resist manipulation.
- **Security by design:** Client tasks and remote participants must be treated as untrusted.
- **Economic clarity:** Virtual credits must never be confused with withdrawable money.

## 8. Current product stage

The project is in brainstorming. No implementation stack, hosting model, task protocol, currency model, legal structure, or MVP scope has been selected.
